# Plan: the remaining gaps to Tom's brief

Written 24 November 2026. A plan only: nothing here is built yet.

I read the brief again from its first line to its last, this time looking for what it promises that I had not yet counted, not only at the 39 sub-questions. The product stands at about 8.5 out of 10 for hair removal by my own rating. This plan lists every remaining difference I found, what would close it, and the order I would do it in.

## What the fresh read found

The earlier reviews held the product against the five questions and the eight measures. Reading the whole brief again turned up nine things outside those lists.

1. **The brief ends with a "next step" we have not delivered.** It says: "run the full set end to end on one live dataset as the worked example". The dashboard does the running. Nobody can yet hand Tom or a client the worked example itself: one document that walks the brief's own structure with real Venus answers in it.
2. **The three outcomes are not shown as outcomes.** The brief opens with salience, purchase intent and better products, and defines salience as talked about more, by more creators, in more of the conversations that matter. We show the parts. We do not show the three outcomes as the headline.
3. **"Brands, markets and months compare like for like."** Brands compare in one table and months in the Reports page. Markets do not: there is a filter, but no table of the eight measures market by market.
4. **Each leader "asks something different first".** The brief names three buyers: the brand lead, the data lead and the growth lead, and says what each gets. We have one summary for everyone.
5. **The top 10 creator lists are not ranked by the score.** The brief's output is a top 10 per theme, product and competitor "scored on fit, performance, buying signals, audience match, cost and availability". Our lists per theme and per product are ranked by views, and per rival by engagement. Only the overall list uses the creator score.
6. **Export and Share do nothing yet.** The brand lead is promised content briefs. A brief that cannot leave the page is not delivered.
7. **The paid loop stops at "we acted on it".** The brief's loop is insight, content, paid media, measured result, next brief. We record that a theme was acted on. We do not record which creators and whether paid ran, so the result cannot be tied to what was done.
8. **The paid audience has hashtags but not the audience's own words.** The brief asks for "interest and keyword sets built from the conversation's language".
9. **The purchase intent headline is not "against the category".** The brief's formula says per 1,000 comments, against the category. The headline tile compares with the period before. The category comparison is one box lower.

## The plan

### Part A: build now, on the data we hold

In the order I would do them.

**A1. The worked example.** One shareable document, built from the live Venus numbers, that follows the brief heading by heading: the idea, the eight measures, each of the five questions with its answer and proof, the three lenses, the loop, the limits. It is written once by a script from the dashboard's own queries, so it cannot disagree with the dashboard, and can be made again each month. This is the brief's own stated next step, and the thing Tom can put in front of a client.

**A2. The three outcomes on top.** A short band above the eight measures: salience (share of voice, number of creators, earned share, and in how many winning themes we hold at least our share), purchase intent (ours against the category), and better products (what to fix first and what people wish existed). Each with its direction since the period before.

**A3. A summary for each leader.** Three tabs on the summary page, in the brief's words: brand lead (where we are losing share, love and hate against rivals, white space), growth lead (intent ranking, creator shortlist, paid audience, product priorities), data lead (coverage, accuracy, fixed norms, the method). Each tab is a selection of boxes that already exist.

**A4. The eight measures by market.** One table, markets as rows, the eight measures as columns, for the five largest markets. Cells under the minimum sample stay empty.

**A5. Top 10 lists by creator score.** Rank the lists per theme, per product and per rival by the creator score, and show its parts beside it.

**A6. Export and share.** Export the summary and any brief as a PDF, and the tables as a spreadsheet. A read-only share link for a dashboard.

**A7. Close the loop on paid.** When a theme is marked as acted on, record which creators were briefed and whether paid ran. The monthly read then reports on those creators' posts and that theme: posts, engagement vs norm, buying signals.

**A8. The remaining sub-question partials.** Six are answered only in part, and five of them can be finished on data we hold:
- under-served audiences by age and gender, from creators whose audience is known (thin: about one creator in six);
- under-served platforms by theme: our share of each theme on each platform against rivals;
- creator credibility: add months active and the tone of the comments under their posts;
- what people wish existed, split by product line;
- variants people ask for, as its own list;
- "is it getting worse": a momentum figure beside each product's objections.

**A9. Keywords from the audience's own words.** Add the phrases commenters use most in each theme to the paid targeting line of each brief, beside the hashtags.

**A10. Purchase intent against the category in the headline.** Show the category rate beside ours in the tile.

**A11. Speed on live.** The summary took 18 seconds from cold on live. Load the answers first and the rest as the reader scrolls, and keep the last result of each box for a few minutes.

**A12. Second readings for one more industry's posts.** Themes for the other nine industries are first-pass only. Run the stricter read on the largest one and measure it.

### Part B: needs Tom or IMAI

**Data**
- **A culture export.** One community or trend, one market, one period. It unlocks the third lens and four sub-questions. The largest single gap.
- **A category-wide export.** "Share of category" and the 25% white-space rule then work as the brief writes them.
- **Comments and full captions for a second industry.** Two of the five questions exist for hair removal only.
- **Transcripts and visual tagging.** The brief promises format and hook analysis from the video itself.
- **Audience data for more creators, with cities.** Audience match reaches about one creator in six, and has no city.
- **Launch dates.** For launch reads at 48 hours and 30 days.
- **Paid media results.** To finish the loop with a measured result.
- **An API or MCP route** to the data, which the brief promises the data lead. I can build a read-only export, but access to the underlying data is IMAI's to give.

**Decisions**
- Earned share: the brief leaves out brand-owned and disclosed paid. We also leave out gifted, affiliate and platform-flagged.
- The white-space thresholds, and whether "unclaimed" counts.
- The creator score weights.
- "Fast growth" for a peaking theme, set at 50%.
- Venus's target audience.

**Reviews**
- The theme list, the product lines and the brand-owned accounts.
- A person outside the build checking a sample. Every accuracy figure is one model judging another.

## What it would do to the rating

By my own estimate:
- A1 to A5 take hair removal from about 8.5 to about 9. They close the gaps between what the brief says a client gets and what a client can pick up.
- A6 to A12 do not move the headline much. They make it usable and finish the partials.
- The culture export is worth more than everything in Part A. Without it the three lenses stay at two.

## Order

1. A1, the worked example.
2. A2 and A3, the outcomes and the three leaders' summaries.
3. A4 and A5, markets side by side and the scored top 10 lists.
4. A6 and A7, export and the paid loop.
5. A8 to A12.

Each step is checked the way the rest was: the number check extended to the new figures, the template check for every industry, a browser walk, and a light pass on live.
